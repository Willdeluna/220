Core Concept

A220 electrical system = smart power network→Automatically selects sources, transfers buses, sheds loads, and protects itself.core idea
You do not manually control most power flow→The system decides routing; maintenance needs to understand the logic behind the decision.
Troubleshooting starts with power path logic→Follow source → bus → controller → load → protection.MX mindset
🧩

System Architecture

Three main channels→Left channel EPC 1, right channel EPC 2, and emergency channel EPC 3.
BPCU 1 and 2 manage distribution→They handle source switching, bus transfers, load shedding, and fault isolation.
EMPC controls emergency power→Emergency logic manages RAT deployment and essential bus routing.emergency brain
⚡

Power Sources

AC power is primary→Main sources are engine VFGs, APU generator, and external power.
DC power comes from AC→TRUs convert AC power to 28V DC for DC buses and essential systems.
RAT is emergency backup→The ram air turbine is the last-line backup when normal AC power is lost.backup
🔄

Variable Frequency Generators

VFGs are engine-driven→Frequency changes with engine speed; no constant-speed drive like older systems.
GCU controls generator operation→Generator control includes regulation, monitoring, and protection.
Generator trips are usually protection→Overvoltage, undervoltage, frequency faults, overcurrent, or differential faults can trip it.protective shutdown
🛡️

OPU Backup Protection

OPU adds hardware backup→It gives independent protection separate from GCU logic.
Two protection layers→Software/control protection plus hardware backup reduces damage risk.
Do not assume a bad generator immediately→A trip may be the system protecting itself from abnormal conditions.line clue
🏢

Electrical Power Centers

EPC = physical routing hub→Contains buses, contactors, TRUs, and distribution hardware.
Power is routed inside EPCs→This is where source power is physically connected to buses and loads.
Separation barriers limit damage spread→The design helps contain fire or fault damage.safety design
🚌

Bus System

Main buses distribute power→AC BUS 1/2, AC ESS BUS, DC BUS 1/2, and DC ESS buses feed aircraft loads.
Normal operation is split bus→Left and right sides normally operate independently.
Tie logic connects sides during failures→Unexpected routing can be normal automatic protection behavior.important
🧠

BPCU Logic

BPCU = decision maker→Controls source switching, power routing, load shedding, and fault isolation.
It responds automatically→The airplane may reconfigure before the crew or mechanic sees the full symptom.
CAS and synoptic pages are key→Use indications to understand what the BPCU commanded.use displays
📉

Load Management

Load shedding is priority based→Non-essential loads drop first when demand is too high or power is limited.
Galleys and IFE are common shed loads→Loss of comfort systems may be normal load management, not a failed component.
Loss of a system may be intentional→The system protects essential loads by removing lower-priority ones.don’t chase ghosts
🔋

DC Power & Batteries

TRUs convert AC to 28V DC→Three TRUs support DC distribution and essential DC power.
TRUs protect themselves→Overtemperature, fan failure, and overvoltage can trip a TRU.
Two NiCad batteries support backup power→Batteries feed emergency/essential loads and can support APU start.survival power
⚡

No-Break Power & SSPCs

SSRPCs provide instant switching support→They help prevent avionics or FBW resets during source transfer.
SSPCs replace many traditional CB functions→They switch, protect, monitor, and report electronically.
CB page matters on modern aircraft→Do not expect every issue to be solved with old-school physical breaker pulling.modern MX
🧰

CDC Distribution Cabinets

CDCs support software-driven distribution→Master CDCs control logic while satellite CDCs follow commands.
SSPC logic can be simple or advanced→Some loads require combinational logic and time delays.
Example: wing anti-ice logic→A system may need multiple valid inputs before power is applied.logic based
🕹️

Fly-By-Wire Power

FBW must never lose power→Dedicated FBW power paths keep flight controls alive.
FBWPC provides dedicated power→Backups include DC bus, PMAG, and battery support.
PMAG supports FBW/EEC power→Even after major generator loss, critical control power remains available.critical
🚨

Emergency Electrical System

RAT deploys if both AC buses fail→It provides emergency AC essential power and hydraulic pressure.
Deployment logic is conditional→RAT deploys in flight above about 50 knots.
EMPC manages emergency routing→When things go bad, EMPC controls essential power distribution.last line
🔥

Protection & Smoke Isolation

Differential protection detects wiring faults→The system isolates bad sections before damage spreads.
Bus tie protection limits fault propagation→The airplane tries to protect the healthy side from the failed side.
BUS ISOL can intentionally cut power→During smoke/fire events, power removal may be commanded—not a failure.critical insight
🔧

Maintenance Reality

Most issues are not failed generators→Often the problem is contactors, sensors, SSPCs, or control logic.
Use synoptic, CAS, and BPCU status→Those tell you what the aircraft thinks is happening.
Lost power may be reconfiguration→Treat it as a system event before calling a component bad.MX takeaway
