Core Concept — ATA 29 Big Picture

A220 hydraulics are triple-redundant→HYD 1, HYD 2, and HYD 3 are independent systems that protect flight control capability.
One system can still support all flight axes→The airplane is designed so a single hydraulic failure does not remove control.big takeaway
Think pressure management, not just pumps→Logic decides when pumps run, valves shift, and priority loads get protected.
🏗️

System Architecture

Each system has its own components→Reservoir, pumps, filters, valves, and monitoring stay separated between systems.
Loads are distributed across the airplane→Flight controls, gear, thrust reversers, spoilers, flaps, and slats are split intelligently.
No single failure should take everything→That is the whole design philosophy of the hydraulic layout.
⚙️

Pump Types — EDP vs ACMP

EDP = engine driven pump→Main source for HYD 1 and HYD 2 anytime the related engine is running.
ACMP = electric motor pump→Backup/assist pump, and primary power source for HYD 3.easy memory
Variable displacement pumps meet demand→They adjust output instead of simply pushing constant flow all the time.
🔀

System Differences

HYD 1: EDP 1A primary→Backup support can come from the PTU and electric pump logic.
HYD 2: EDP 2A primary→ACMP 2B gives backup pressure when required.
HYD 3: electrically driven→ACMP 3A/3B supply it, with RAT backup in emergency conditions.key difference
📈

Pressure System Basics

Normal pressure is about 3000 psi→This is the working pressure target for hydraulic users.
Pressure switches drive logic→Pressure values are used for pump commands, warnings, and system state.
Low pressure does not always mean failed pump→Quantity, temperature, priority valves, logic, or electrical shedding can be involved.
🛢️

Bootstrap Reservoir

Reservoir is self-pressurizing→It uses hydraulic pressure to maintain pump inlet pressure.
Prevents cavitation→The pump needs positive inlet pressure to avoid damage and pressure loss.
Reservoir is active, not just storage→It helps the system work through negative G and aggressive maneuvering.unique concept
🔋

Accumulators

Accumulators store hydraulic energy→They support transient demand and short-term pressure needs.
HYD 3 has extra accumulator support→This matters during RAT transition and emergency operation.
Think short-term backup pressure→They are not the main power source, but they smooth and protect the system.
🔄

PTU — Power Transfer Unit

PTU transfers power, not fluid→HYD 2 can help HYD 1 mechanically while fluid stays separated.
Automatic during specific conditions→Low HYD 1 pressure, takeoff, approach, and single-engine taxi can trigger it.
PTU running may be normal→Do not call it a failure just because the system is helping itself.MX clue
🚦

Priority Valves

Flight controls get priority→When pressure drops, non-essential users are limited first.
Loss of some users can be protection logic→Priority valves explain why one system function may disappear while controls remain.
Pressure is rationed by importance→This is hydraulic load shedding, airplane style.
🌀

RAT Emergency Backup

RAT powers HYD 3 in emergency→It gives essential hydraulic power when normal electrical power is lost.
Deploys with major electrical failure logic→Loss of both AC buses can trigger RAT deployment in flight.
Electrical failure can create hydraulic backup action→This is why ATA 24 and ATA 29 are tightly connected.cross ATA
🔥

Shutdown & Overheat Protection

FWSOV isolates engine hydraulic supply→The firewall shutoff valve cuts fluid flow to the EDP when required.
Fire and overheat can close valves→Protection may look like a hydraulic system fault if you miss the trigger logic.
Temperature protection comes first→Sensors, switches, and thermal devices protect the system before damage spreads.
❄️

Cooling & Filters

HYD 1 and 2 use fuel cooling→Hydraulic temperature can tie back to fuel cooling performance.
HYD 3 cools differently→Air convection and radiation handle its thermal control.
Filters are high-value maintenance items→Pressure, return, and case drain filters can create real write-ups.common check
⚡

ACMP & Electrical Interaction

ACMPs respond to logic→Takeoff, approach, engine failure, and high demand can command pump operation.
ACMPs can be load shed→If electrical power is limited, hydraulic assist may be affected.
Hydraulic symptoms can start in ATA 24→Always consider electrical availability when electric pumps behave strangely.
📺

Indications & Synoptic

HYD synoptic is your primary tool→Check pressure, quantity, temperature, pump state, and valve positions.
CAS tells you where to start→Use messages to guide the path, not to blindly swap parts.
Compare displayed state to expected logic→Is the pump supposed to be on? Is the valve supposed to be closed?line MX
🔧

Maintenance Reality

Most faults are not total system failures→Sensors, pressure/temperature/quantity inputs, pump logic, and valves are common.
Pumps are not always the villain→Pressure loss can be caused by logic, fluid quantity, valve state, or power.
Follow pressure and logic together→Hydraulics are physical pressure controlled by smart system decisions.
🧭

Troubleshooting Flow

Step 1: Check HYD synoptic→Start with pressure, quantity, temperature, pump status, and valve position.
Step 2: Check pump source→Separate EDP, ACMP, PTU, and RAT logic before calling a component bad.
Step 3: Check protection logic→Priority valves, FWSOVs, thermal protection, and load shedding explain many symptoms.
Step 4: Go component-level last→After logic makes sense, check pump, valve, sensor, filter, or fluid issue.pressure → flow → logic → component
