Core Concept — What ATA 30 Really Is

Automatic ice detection + protection system→Detects icing and automatically protects engines, wings, sensors, windows, and visibility.core idea
Logic-driven, not just manual anti-ice→The system reacts to detection signals, controller logic, pressure, and temperature.
Two types of protection power→Bleed air handles engine/wing anti-ice; electrical power handles probes, windows, and wipers.big picture
❄️

Ice Detection System

Vibrating probe detects ice→Ice buildup changes the probe frequency, then the heater melts it and the cycle repeats.
Ice detection talks to multiple systems→Signals go to EECs, IASCs, and PFCC logic — not just anti-ice valves.important
Detection affects flight controls too→Ice signals can change stall/protection logic, so it is bigger than just warm air.MX clue
⚠️

Ice Detection Failure Logic

Fail-safe behavior assumes icing→If detector data is invalid, the aircraft treats conditions as icing to protect itself.fail-safe
ICE DET FAIL points to detection path→Start with detector status, signals, and associated controller logic before chasing valves.
System protects first→A “weird” anti-ice activation can be normal protection logic, not a random failure.
🔥

Engine Anti-Ice — CAIS

Uses engine bleed air→Bleed air from the compressor heats the engine inlet/cowl area.
Each engine is independent→Left and right CAIS can operate separately based on their own control and monitoring.
CAIV valves are fail-safe open→Loss of power tends toward protection, so the valve opens rather than leaving the inlet unprotected.key safety
🧩

CAIS Control Logic

Controlled by the EEC→Pilot selection is not direct valve movement; EEC logic commands the system.
AUTO follows ice detection→In AUTO, CAIS activates when icing is detected and conditions allow.
ON forces protection→ON commands anti-ice regardless of normal detection logic, with condition-based inhibits still important.
🌡️

CAIS Monitoring & Fault Clues

Temperature monitors overheat→Hot duct or leak conditions can generate nacelle overheat indications.
Pressure verifies airflow→Low or high pressure faults point toward flow, valve, or duct problems.
COWL A/ICE FAIL is usually pressure/logic→Do not assume the valve is bad until pressure, temp, and command state are checked.troubleshooting
🪽

Wing Anti-Ice — WAIS

Protects selected slats only→WAIS heats slats 2, 3, and 4 — not the entire wing.
Bleed air flows through piccolo tubes→Hot air enters the protected slat zones and exhausts overboard.
Controlled by IASCs→ATA 21 controllers are heavily involved in ATA 30 wing anti-ice operation.cross ATA
🎛️

WAIS Logic & Inhibits

AUTO turns on with ice detection→WAIS activates in flight when icing is detected, then turns off after icing stops.
Takeoff and ground inhibit logic matters→The system may intentionally stay off to protect performance or because conditions do not allow operation.
ON forces the request→Even with switch ON, controller conditions and protections still decide final behavior.
🚨

WAIS Protection & Leak Logic

Temperature + pressure are the key checks→High temp, low heat, and low pressure messages point toward flow or thermal problems.
Leak detection shuts system off→The system protects structure first if a bleed leak is suspected.
Cross-bleed can protect both wings→If one engine source is lost, cross-bleed logic can keep wing protection available.redundancy
⚙️

IASCs — WAIS Brain

Two IASCs provide redundancy→Multi-channel logic enables, disables, monitors, and diagnoses WAIS operation.
Controls pressure and temperature monitoring→The IASC decides whether the heat being commanded is actually valid.
ATA 21 faults can look like ATA 30 faults→Do not ignore ECS controller status when troubleshooting wing anti-ice.MX clue
📍

Probe Heat System

Protects air data and AOA sensors→AOA vanes, air data probes, and TAT probes are electrically heated.
Ground heat is lower / regulated→On the ground, heating is controlled to prevent damage while still protecting sensors.
Flight heat is full power→In flight the system increases heat because air data reliability is safety critical.safety critical
✋

Probe Heat Maintenance Hazard

Probes get extremely hot→Always verify power is off and allow cooling before touching probes.warning
Usually automatic→Probe heat normally comes on with engines running or when airborne.
Bad probe heat can corrupt air data→A probe heat issue may show up as air data or flight control behavior, not just ATA 30.
🪟

Window Heat System

Windshields are anti-ice + defog→Windshields are heated for visibility and icing protection.
Side windows are mostly defog→Side windows run lower heat for visibility support.
WIPC controllers manage heat→Controllers use sensors and protection logic to avoid thermal damage.
🧠

Window Heat Logic

Always on when powered→The system is designed to maintain window readiness automatically.
Cold start reduces power→Thermal protection prevents shocking the glass with too much heat too quickly.
Three sensors per window→One controls, two monitor; if all fail, the system shuts down heat.
🌧️

Windshield Wipers

Left and right are independent→Each side has its own electrically powered wiper system.
Auto park and synchronization built in→The system manages position and smooth operation automatically.
No CAS for many wiper failures→Use BITE/OMS and direct checks because EICAS may not tell the whole story.MX clue
🔗

System Integration

ATA 36 supplies bleed air→Bleed pressure and temperature issues can create anti-ice faults.
ATA 21 controls WAIS through IASCs→ECS controller logic is part of the ice protection chain.
ATA 27 can change stall logic→Ice detection can affect flight-control protection behavior.
ATA 24 powers electrical heat→Probe heat, windows, controllers, and wipers depend on electrical supply.
🔧

Troubleshooting Flow

Start with the CAS message→Identify whether the fault is CAIS, WAIS, probe heat, window heat, or wipers.step 1
Check the AIR/synoptic page→Look at valve position, pressure, temperature, and command state.step 2
Follow detection → logic → airflow→Do not jump straight to the valve until controller inputs make sense.step 3
Verify pressure/temp sensors→Most faults are sensors, valves, pressure, temperature, or logic — not total system failure.step 4
