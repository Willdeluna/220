Core Concept

A220 ATA 32 is a hybrid system: hydraulic/mechanical landing gear plus fully electric brake-by-wire.→Gear uses hydraulics and structure; brakes use computers and electric actuators.
Line MX takeaway→Separate mechanical, hydraulic, electrical, and logic problems first.ATA 32
🧩

System Architecture

Three major subsystems: landing gear, nose wheel steering, and brakes.→LGSCU controls gear and steering; BDCU controls braking logic.
Line MX takeaway→Different computers control different parts of ATA 32.ATA 32
🛬

Landing Gear Overview

Tricycle gear: two main landing gears and one nose landing gear.→MLG retracts inward; NLG retracts forward.
Line MX takeaway→Hydraulics move the gear, but logic controls when it moves.ATA 32
⚙️

LGSCU Control Logic

LGSCU is the landing gear brain with redundant control.→Inputs include gear lever, uplock/downlock sensors, and WOW sensors.
Line MX takeaway→Gear movement is logic-controlled, not just hydraulic.ATA 32
⚠️

Weight-On-Wheels

WOW proximity sensors tell the aircraft air vs ground status.→WOW drives gear retraction, brakes, steering, and many other systems.
Line MX takeaway→Bad WOW signals can create multiple unrelated-looking faults.ATA 32
🔄

Gear Retraction Sequence

Lever UP commands the LGSCU, unlock actuator breaks the downlock, retraction actuator moves the gear, and uplock confirms position.→If the sequence does not confirm correctly, the system stops or flags a fault.
Line MX takeaway→Follow the sequence before blaming the actuator.ATA 32
⬇️

Gear Extension Sequence

Lever DOWN releases uplock, gear extends, side brace locks overcenter, and downlock springs hold the gear.→Mechanical lock holds the gear down, not hydraulic pressure.
Line MX takeaway→Structure keeps it safe after extension.ATA 32
🪂

Alternate Extension

Alternate extension uses electrical uplock release, gravity, and air loads.→Hydraulic pressure is dumped to return so the gear can free-fall.
Line MX takeaway→No hydraulics required — major safety backup.ATA 32
🔒

Mechanical Locking

Gear locks using side brace, locking stay, and downlock springs.→Overcenter geometry prevents collapse under load.
Line MX takeaway→This is why mechanical condition matters as much as hydraulic pressure.ATA 32
🛞

Shock Struts

Oleopneumatic struts use oil for damping and nitrogen for spring force.→Metering pin controls compression rate during landing.
Line MX takeaway→Low nitrogen or oil shows up as hard landing or poor rebound behavior.ATA 32
📍

Proximity Sensors

Uplock, downlock, and WOW sensing are major ATA 32 trouble areas.→Dual sensors per gear help the system compare and validate position.
Line MX takeaway→Most gear faults are sensor/data faults, not broken gear.ATA 32
🧭

Nose Wheel Steering

NWS is steer-by-wire with no mechanical link.→Tiller gives large steering range; rudder pedals give small steering range.
Line MX takeaway→LGSCU controls steering like a ground version of FBW.ATA 32
🧠

NWS Logic

Steering requires WOW true, gear down, and system ON.→Steering range reduces as speed increases.
Line MX takeaway→Logic prevents oversteering at high taxi speeds.ATA 32
🚧

NWS Protection

Overtravel sensing protects the system during extreme steering angles.→Towing incidents can create steering warnings or sensor problems.
Line MX takeaway→Always check steering limits after abnormal tow events.ATA 32
🛑

Electric Brake System

A220 brakes are fully electric — no hydraulic brake pressure.→BDCU commands, EMCU controls, and EMA applies brake force.
Line MX takeaway→This is brake-by-wire: command → control → apply.ATA 32
🎚️

Brake Modes

Brake modes include normal, alternate, autobrake, park brake, and retract braking.→Protection changes by mode.
Line MX takeaway→Do not assume every mode has the same protections.ATA 32
🛡️

Brake Protections

Software provides antiskid, locked wheel protection, and touchdown protection.→The system controls brake force based on logic and feedback.
Line MX takeaway→Braking faults often come from data/control logic, not the brake stack.ATA 32
📉

Autobrake Logic

Autobrake targets deceleration rates: LO, MED, and HI.→It activates on landing with idle thrust logic.
Line MX takeaway→Autobrake controls decel, not just “brake pressure.”ATA 32
🌡️

Brake Temperature

Brake temperature is shown on status pages with normal/elevated/overheat indications.→Heat is one of the biggest brake threats.
Line MX takeaway→Hot brakes can become a dispatch and safety concern fast.ATA 32
📡

Tire Pressure Monitoring

Wireless TPM sensors send data through aircraft data modules to displays.→Tire pressure monitoring is fully electronic.
Line MX takeaway→Indication faults may be sensor/data path issues.ATA 32
🔥

Wheel Protection

Thermal fuse plugs melt around high wheel temperatures to prevent tire explosion.→They release pressure before tire burst risk becomes severe.
Line MX takeaway→Wheel safety is about controlled release, not holding pressure forever.ATA 32
🔧

Maintenance Reality

Most faults are proximity sensors, BDCU/logic issues, temp indications, or pressure indications.→Structural gear failure is rare.
Line MX takeaway→A220 pattern: data and logic faults dominate.ATA 32
🧪

Troubleshooting Flow

Start with EICAS/CAS, then check synoptic position and status.→Verify WOW/position sensors, then separate hydraulic gear issues from electric brake issues.
Line MX takeaway→Follow input → logic → actuator → mechanical.ATA 32
