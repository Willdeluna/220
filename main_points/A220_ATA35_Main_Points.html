Reusable gaseous oxygen system with monitored pressure, regulators, masks, microphone integration, and emergency positive-pressure modes.

Passenger Oxygen

🧠

Core Concept

ATA 35 = emergency life-support system→It provides oxygen for crew, passengers, lavatories, and portable use.
Used when things go wrong→Depressurization, cockpit smoke/fumes, and medical situations are the big drivers.safety critical
Different users = different oxygen systems→Crew, passenger, lav, and portable systems do not all work the same way.
🧩

System Architecture

Crew oxygen = gaseous cylinder system→Compressed oxygen is stored, regulated, monitored, and delivered to the flight deck masks.
Passenger oxygen = chemical generator system→PSU masks start a chemical oxygen reaction when pulled.one-time use
Lav and portable oxygen have separate designs→Lav oxygen uses bottle logic; portable oxygen supports medical and crew movement needs.
🛢️

Crew Oxygen Storage

Stored as compressed oxygen→Typical bottle pressure is around 1850 psi with a protected cylinder location below the cockpit.
High pressure gets regulated down→High-pressure storage is reduced to low pressure for breathing supply.
Crew oxygen is reusable and monitored→Unlike passenger chemical generators, the crew system can be serviced and reused.
⚠️

Pressure Protection

Relief valves protect the system→High-pressure and low-pressure relief prevent overpressure damage.
ODI disc is a major clue→If the overboard discharge indicator popped, suspect overpressure or oxygen release.MX clue
Oxygen pressure data can mislead you→Bad pressure or temperature transducer data can make the system look low or abnormal.
📊

Crew Oxygen Indications

EICAS shows bottle pressure→Normal pressure indication is white; low pressure becomes amber.
CREW OXY LO PRESS matters→Low pressure means reduced crew protection time.dispatch impact
Powered by essential electrical supply→Monitoring depends on electrical power and sensor health.
🎭

Crew Masks

Quick-donning masks protect and communicate→The mask provides breathing oxygen and microphone capability.
Modes: NORM, 100%, EMERGENCY→NORM mixes oxygen and cabin air; 100% gives pure oxygen; EMERGENCY gives positive pressure.
Emergency mode helps during smoke→Positive pressure helps keep smoke/fumes out of the mask.cockpit smoke
✅

Mask Activation & Test

Opening the stowage box starts flow→The OXY ON flag confirms oxygen flow.
Flow indicator tells the truth→Use the blinker/indicator during test to verify actual flow.
Mic selection integrates with ATA 23→Mask use ties oxygen to communication systems.cross ATA
👥

Passenger Oxygen

Chemical generators in PSUs→Passenger oxygen is produced chemically, not fed from the crew bottle.
Auto deployment by cabin altitude→Masks deploy automatically at high cabin altitude, or manually from the cockpit.
Electrical release, chemical oxygen→Power opens the doors; pulling the mask starts the generator.big distinction
🔥

Chemical Generator Hazards

Once started, it cannot be stopped→Pulling the mask starts the chemical reaction until the generator is spent.
Generators get extremely hot→Treat them as a burn and fire hazard after activation.danger zone
Safety pins matter→Improper servicing can cause accidental activation.
🚻

Lavatory Oxygen

Lav oxygen uses a bottle system→It is different from passenger PSU chemical generators.
Tamper-proof design→Lav oxygen has added protection due to passenger access and fire risk.
Know which oxygen system you are checking→Crew, passenger, lav, and portable oxygen troubleshooting are different paths.
🧑‍⚕️

Portable Oxygen

Used for medical emergencies→Portable bottles provide flexible oxygen away from fixed mask stations.
Supports crew movement→Useful if crew must leave the seat during an oxygen/smoke event.
Check pressure and availability→Missing, low, or improperly serviced portable bottles can affect dispatch.
🔗

Passenger Deployment Integration

Deployment triggers cabin reactions→Seatbelt signs, lights, audio volume, and IFE behavior may change automatically.
Controlled by multiple systems→CDC, IASC, and DMC logic can be involved in deployment decisions.
One oxygen event can look like many faults→Cabin systems reacting together may be normal emergency logic.system logic
🧯

Oxygen System Hazards

Oxygen increases fire risk→Oil, grease, contamination, and rapid pressurization are serious hazards.
Clean handling is mandatory→Keep oxygen fittings and components free from contamination.no shortcuts
Follow servicing procedures exactly→This is one ATA where casual handling can become dangerous fast.
🔧

Maintenance Reality

Crew faults are often pressure/sensor related→Check bottle pressure, regulator, transducer, and indication path.
Passenger faults are often latch/generator related→Check PSU doors, release logic, generator expiry, and installation status.
Complete system failure is rare→Most problems are local to one supply, mask, latch, bottle, or indication.
🛠️

Troubleshooting Flow

Step 1: Check CAS→Start with the message and determine which oxygen system is involved.
Step 2: Identify the system→Crew, passenger, lav, and portable oxygen each troubleshoot differently.
Step 3: Follow supply → control → output→Pressure or generator, valve/latch logic, then mask/output confirmation.line MX flow
