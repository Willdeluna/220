Auxiliary Power Made Simple

A fast interactive study page for the A220 APU: ECU control, start sequence, electrical output, bleed air, fuel/oil systems, inlet door logic, protective shutdowns, and line-maintenance troubleshooting.

⚙️
Self-Contained Engine
The APU is a small gas turbine in the tail that provides electrical power and pneumatic air independently of the main engines.
🧠
ECU Controlled
The ECU runs the start, fuel, ignition, speed, load, protection, and shutdown logic.
🔥
Protects Itself
Many APU “failures” are protective ECU shutdowns caused by unsafe start, oil, temp, speed, sensor, or load conditions.
🧠

Core Concept

ATA 49 = self-contained auxiliary power plant→It supplies electrical power and bleed air while operating independently of the engines.
APU supports ground ops and in-flight redundancy→It can power ECS, engine starts, and electrical backup functions.big picture
Think like an engine→Fuel + air + ignition + control + protection drive most troubleshooting.
🌀

System Architecture

Major sections: power section, load compressor, accessory gearbox→Air is compressed, mixed with fuel, burned, and expanded through the turbine.
Single-shaft turbine drives multiple outputs→One shaft powers the compressor, generator, and gearbox accessories.easy memory
Accessory gearbox also acts as oil reservoir→It powers the FCU, oil pump, generator drive, and supports lubrication storage.
🧠

ECU — APU Brain

ECU has full authority control→It controls start, fuel, ignition, speed, load, shutdown, and protection logic.
Most APU failures are ECU protective actions→The ECU may inhibit start or shut down the APU to prevent damage.key MX idea
Watch the phase of operation→Start, running, loading, and shutdown each have different fault logic.
🔄

Start Sequence

Start begins with BIT and inlet door opening→If the door does not open correctly, start may be inhibited.
Starter, ignition, then fuel introduction→Fuel comes in around low RPM after the ECU confirms start conditions.
Starter disengages when the APU becomes self-sustaining→Watch RPM and EGT for no light-off, hung start, or hot start clues.line clue
⚡

Electrical Output

APU generator powers aircraft buses→APU must reach proper speed before electrical loading is allowed.
GCU controls generator protection→The generator side is managed and protected separately from the turbine core.
ECU sends ready-to-load signal→No ready-to-load means chase APU speed, control logic, or protection status first.
🌬️

Pneumatic / Bleed Output

Load compressor supplies bleed air→Used for packs and engine starts.
BAV controls bleed delivery→Air output depends on demand and ECU-controlled valve logic.
Engine start is high-priority pneumatic demand→APU air control may change behavior during start support.ATA 36 link
🧯

Air System Control

IGV adjusts compressor airflow→More demand opens the inlet guide vanes for more air.
SCV prevents compressor surge→Low demand can open the surge control valve to protect the compressor.
Air system is dynamic, not fixed→APU bleed behavior changes with load and protection needs.
⛽

Fuel System

Fuel comes from the left engine feed line→Fuel supply issues can look like APU start or flameout faults.
FCU filters, pressurizes, and meters fuel→The ECU commands fuel flow electronically through the control system.
Primary and secondary manifolds sequence fuel→Wrong sequencing can trigger shutdown.start logic
🛢️

Oil System

Wet sump oil system lubricates, cools, and cleans→Oil health is critical because the APU is a turbine engine.
Low oil pressure causes shutdown→Oil pressure protection is strict and immediate.
High oil temperature causes shutdown→Oil temperature problems are treated as serious protection events.protective
🚪

Inlet Door Logic

Door opens automatically during start→Without the correct door position, airflow is not available for start.
Different positions for flight and ground→Door position supports airflow needs and aerodynamic conditions.
Door faults are common start-inhibit clues→Check door indication before chasing fuel or ignition.
🚨

Shutdown & Protection

Normal shutdown removes load, cools, then cuts fuel→Cooldown prevents turbine heat damage.
Protective shutdown has no cooldown period→Overspeed, overtemp, low oil pressure, and sensor faults can command immediate shutdown.critical
Most shutdowns protect the machine→Do not treat every shutdown as a failed APU core.
📊

Indications & Interfaces

Status page shows RPM, EGT, door position, and oil parameters→Use these as the quick health check.
APU links to ATA 24, 36, 21, and 45→Electrical, bleed air, ECS, and OMS faults can all connect back to ATA 49.
APU problems may look like ECS or electrical faults→Always check what load the APU was carrying.cross-system
🔧

Maintenance / Troubleshooting Flow

Step 1 — Check CAS message→Start with what the aircraft is telling you.
Step 2 — Check RPM, EGT, and oil→These tell you if the problem is start, combustion, temperature, or lubrication.
Step 3 — Identify the phase→Was it during start, running, loading, or shutdown?
Step 4 — Check fuel, electrical, and air support systems→Follow: Start → control → load → shutdown.use this
